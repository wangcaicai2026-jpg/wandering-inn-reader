CHAPTER 01 · PART 01

The Empty Inn

空荡荡的旅店 · A1–A2 情节保留版
阅读进度 0%

轻触任意英文句子，在单词正下方展开中文释义

The inn was dark and empty when a young woman came to it. It stood alone on a high hill, above fields of grass. The autumn grass moved in the wind. Some of it was green, some was orange, and some was purple.

From far away, the hills looked soft and gentle. But the land was not easy to cross. There were hills and low places everywhere. A traveller could walk for hours and lose the way. Even from the top of a hill, it was hard to know where to go.

Huge mountains stood around the land. Their tops disappeared into the clouds. Between them was one narrow way from north to south. There were no towns or houses in sight. That was why the old inn was so important to a lost traveller.

The inn was on one of the highest hills. Long ago, people could see its roof from far away. They could come here to rest and find help. Its roof was old, and paint was coming off its window shutters. But the building still stood.

For many years, the world around the inn had been dangerous. Armies had come through this land. Later, the undead had come, too. People left the inn, came back, and left it again. Now nobody came. The windows stayed dark.

Still, something special remained inside the old walls. People had worked hard to build this place. They had even used special Skills and magic to make it strong. The inn had been empty for years, but it had not fallen down. It was still waiting.

The young woman did not know any of this. She only knew that this was the first building she had seen in this strange world. To her, an inn meant people, food, rest, and safety. It was a place where a tired traveller could finally stop running.

But she was not walking like a normal traveller. She pushed through the tall grass, breathing hard. She kept looking behind her. She had run as fast as she could, but now she could barely walk. She smelled of smoke, and part of her shirt was black from fire.

Her right arm was burned. There were cuts on her legs. Every step hurt, but she kept going. She had seen a Dragon, and small monsters with red eyes had chased her. She did not know if they were still behind her.

She climbed the hill and looked at the dark windows. There was no light inside. Her hope grew smaller, but she went to the door anyway. "Hello?" she called. "Is... is anyone here?" Nobody answered.

She looked back at the strange hills. Night was coming, and there were no electric lights anywhere. Outside were the monsters that had almost killed her. She was not sure the inn would be safe, either. But staying outside seemed even worse.

She pushed the door open. "Anyone here? Hello? I need—" She stopped speaking. The air inside was old and full of dust. It smelled like a library that nobody had opened for years. The young woman began to cough.

"No. Oh, come on! Of course it's empty." She leaned against the door and sighed. Old tables and chairs stood in the dark, covered with dust and spiderwebs. Nobody had used this room for a very long time. Her strength seemed to leave her all at once.

She touched her burned arm and cried out in pain. The cuts on her legs hurt, too. She wanted to cry, but she tried not to. She had known this place might be empty. Still, she had hoped to find someone. "It's not fair," she said. "Ever since I came to this world, everything has gone wrong."

Speaking out loud helped her a little. The main room was huge, with space for many people. But now it was silent and almost completely dark. She felt small, as if she did not belong there. Her steps made a sound that seemed to disappear into the empty room.

She stopped and looked behind her again. What if one of those monsters came back? She had no idea where she was or how to get home. She was hurt, alone, and in a world she did not understand. "I just wanted to go to the bathroom," she whispered.

For a moment she thought about leaving. Then she heard a sound above her and jumped. She ran back through the door, straight into cold rain. Water hit her face. The rain became heavier and heavier, so she hurried inside again.

Soon, the rain was so loud that she could hardly hear anything else. Water came through cracks in the roof and fell onto the dusty floor. Outside, she could see almost nothing. But inside the inn, something changed. For the first time, the girl felt a little peaceful.

Maybe the Dragon would not come out in this rain. Maybe the little green monsters could not find her now. She might be safe here, at least for a while. Then she saw an old chair lying on its side. It was dirty, but it looked wonderful to her.

She stood the chair up, and more dust flew into the air. She coughed and sneezed, then brushed the chair clean with her hand. At last she sat down. She closed her eyes and tried to rest. Her name was Erin Solstice, and she was far from home.

She wanted to think about what had happened, but she was too tired. Slowly, the fear began to leave her body. She was almost asleep when a new thought came to her. Erin opened one eye, then the other. She looked at the empty inn and sighed. "I'm really hungry."

After a few minutes, Erin sat up again. She was the first guest this inn had seen in a very long time. But she was not really a traveller. She had not wanted to go anywhere tonight. Just a short time ago, she had been at home.

She had finished a game of chess in her room. Then she had gone to the bathroom. Or she had tried to. One step should have taken her through the bathroom door. Instead, she had suddenly been standing in a cave.

There was no bright light or strange voice to warn her. One moment she was at home; the next, she was somewhere else. And right in front of her was a sleeping Dragon. A real Dragon. Erin jumped up from the chair as she remembered it.

"It wasn't a dream!" She looked down at her right arm. The burn still hurt terribly. "But why am I burned? I thought the fire missed me!" The Dragon had sent a great line of purple fire through the cave.

The flames had not touched her directly. Still, the heat was so strong that it burned her right side. Her jeans protected part of her leg, but her arm had no protection. Erin did not know how she had moved out of the way. She only knew that she was lucky to be alive.

She had run out of the cave and into the strange hills. She did not know where she was. She called for help and shouted as loudly as she could. That was a terrible mistake. Her shouting did not bring people. It brought monsters.

Small green creatures had heard her. They had red eyes and mouths full of sharp teeth. They chased Erin across the grass and cut at her legs. She kept running and screaming until she finally escaped. Now she could still feel every cut.

"What were those things?" she asked the empty room. "Goblins? They looked like Goblins." But Goblins were supposed to be in stories and games. Dragons were supposed to be there, too. How could any of this be real?

Erin touched her arm again. The pain was real, and she could feel the dust in her throat. This place could not be a dream. But it made no sense. "What is this place?" she asked. "An inn? Really?"

She looked around more carefully. There were wooden tables, old chairs, and a long bar. A staircase led to the second floor. The windows had wooden shutters, but no glass. She saw no plastic and no electric lights. It looked like a building from a very old time.

For a moment, Erin imagined the inn full of people. Someone might stand behind the bar and give her a cold drink. People could sit at the tables, talk, and laugh. She almost smiled at the thought. Then dust filled her throat, and she began coughing again.

This time, the coughing was so bad that she had to go to the open door for fresh air. Her eyes filled with tears. The Dragon had hurt her. The Goblins had hurt her. Now the dust seemed to be trying to kill her, too. "This place is going to kill me!" she cried.

"Someone needs to clean this place!" Erin looked around. Nobody answered, of course. She took a long breath and sighed. "And I guess that someone is me."

If she stayed here all night, she needed to breathe. She could not keep coughing every minute. What if monsters heard the noise? The inn had walls and a roof, and it might be her best place to stay. But first she had to make it possible to sleep there.

Erin stopped and thought about her situation. Where was she? Why was she here? How could she ever go home? Fear rose inside her again. She wanted to scream, but she quickly closed her mouth. Something outside might hear her, even through the rain.

"Don't scream. Don't panic. Think," she told herself. She sat at a table and tried to make a plan. "First, I stay here. Then I look around. And I find something to clean with." She tried to sit up straight, like someone making an important decision.

Erin had just been playing chess at home. So she thought about the first moves in a game. "Pawn to E4. Pawn to C5. Sicilian Defense," she said. It was a strange thing to say here, but it helped her think. "All right. Here we go."

She checked her pockets before exploring the inn. In stories, people often had useful things for their adventures. Erin had nothing. No phone, no bag, no food, and certainly no weapon. She had only come to use the bathroom. Why would she have packed anything?

She walked around the main room, looking for something useful. Everywhere she looked, she found more dust. There had been no warning before she came to this world. No magic door that she could see, and no voice telling her what to do. Was she in a game? Was she going crazy?

Then she remembered the Dragon and the Goblins. Perhaps this really was a world with magic. In that case, maybe she needed a sword. Or a magic wand. Or even a magic sword. Erin thought for a moment and looked at the dusty tables. "No. First, I need a dustrag—a cloth for cleaning."

That was her first task in this strange world: find a dustrag and make a clean place to sit. It was not the kind of adventure she had imagined. But it was something she could do right now.

Erin began by looking around the main room. She checked the tables and chairs, but they were only covered with dust. Behind the long bar, she found a line of old, dirty mugs. There was no cleaning cloth there. Then she saw a door behind the bar.

She opened the door and looked inside. It was a kitchen. There were cupboards, tables for cutting food, and a stone stove. The stove used fire, not gas or electricity. There was also a huge old oven for baking bread.

At least the kitchen had some useful things. Erin saw a pot, a heavy pan, and some old tools. One of the tools was covered with rust. Then she saw something else beside them. "Aha! A bucket!" She picked it up and smiled.

A bucket was not a cloth, but she would need water to clean. And outside, the rain was still falling hard. She carried the bucket to the door and set it out in the rain. Soon it was full of clean water. Erin also put some old mugs outside to catch more.

Now she had water, but still no cloth. She opened the kitchen cupboards one by one. Inside were old plates, tools, and things that had fallen apart. She could not find any cloth or towels. "Oh, come on," she said.

She searched the rest of the ground floor. There was nothing she could use to wipe the tables. She looked at her own shirt, then shook her head. It was already burned, and she did not want to use it. There was only one place left to search.

Upstairs. Erin turned to the staircase. The steps were wooden, old, and very dark. In the shadows, the stairs almost looked like the bones of a huge monster. Erin swallowed and put one foot on the first step. Finding a cloth was becoming much harder than she had expected.

The first step made a loud creaking sound. Erin stopped at once. She listened carefully, but nothing moved. Then she took another step. The stairs creaked again, and the sound filled the empty inn.

"Come on. Come on. You can do this," Erin whispered. She tried to keep her voice low. What if something was sleeping upstairs? Her heart beat faster. She stopped halfway up the stairs and looked into the darkness.

"This is stupid. There is nothing up there," she told herself. But then another thought came. What if there were more Goblins? What if a Dragon was waiting there? She actually took one step back down.

"No, that is silly. How could a Dragon fit upstairs?" She gave a small nervous laugh. "But Goblins could fit." The shadows did not move, but she was still scared. It was the kind of fear she had felt as a child in a dark room.

Erin knew there were probably no monsters upstairs. If there were, they might already have come after her. But knowing that did not make the darkness feel safe. She needed to find a cloth, and she did not want to give up.

"Dustrag. Dustrag. Dustrag," she whispered. The words gave her something simple to think about. She took one step, then another. Every loud noise made her heart jump, but nothing attacked her. At last she reached the second floor.

Upstairs was even darker than she had expected. She could barely see a few steps ahead. The long hall was full of shadows. Erin wanted to run back down, but she had come too far. "Dustrag," she whispered again, and walked on.

She opened the door to the first room. It was very dark inside. Suddenly she heard a soft sound. Rustle, rustle. Erin stopped moving. Was there something in the room with her?

She listened. The rain was still falling outside, and wind was blowing against the inn. Maybe the sound was only a leaf moving near the window. Then she heard it again. Rustle, rustle. This time she was sure.

Something was moving in the dark. It sounded almost like wings opening. Erin's heart was beating so fast that it hurt. She wanted the sound to be a small rat. But what if it was something much bigger? "Dustrag, dustrag, dustrag..." she whispered faster.

Thunder sounded far away. Then a strong wind hit the inn. Something white suddenly opened wide and flew toward Erin. She screamed and threw her arms up. The white thing hit her face and wrapped around her head and arms.

Erin fell to the floor. Rain blew in through the open window and hit her face. She pushed and pulled at the thing, sure that a monster had caught her. At last she threw it away and jumped to her feet. She looked at the terrible creature.

It was a curtain. Just an old white curtain, moving in the wind. Erin stood still for a few seconds, too shocked to speak. Her heart was still beating wildly. "Curtains," she finally said. "Of course. Curtains."

She picked up the old curtain. It had once been white, but now it was grey with dirt. Still, it was a piece of cloth. Erin had come upstairs to find a cleaning cloth, and now she had one. After all that fear, the answer was right in her hands.

She closed the window, and the strange noise stopped. The room was still dark, and she had no wish to explore more. She hurried back downstairs to the room she knew. It was dusty, but at least it felt familiar. Erin put the curtain next to her bucket. "All right," she said. "Now I can clean."

Erin looked around the huge main room. Almost everything was covered with a thick layer of dust. "Where should I start?" she asked. She chose the table where she had been sitting. She only wanted one clean place to rest.

She put the old curtain into the bucket of rainwater. Then she pulled the wet cloth across the table. A great cloud of dust flew up. Erin jumped back, coughing. She waited for the air to clear, then tried again.

This time she moved the wet cloth more carefully. The dust came off the table and fell to the floor. She wiped the wood again and again. Soon she could see its dark brown color. Under all that dust, the table was still good.

The water in the bucket quickly turned grey. When it became too dirty, Erin threw it outside. She left the bucket in the rain until it was full again. There was plenty of fresh water, at least. She returned to the table and finished cleaning it.

"One table done!" she said happily. Then she looked around. There were so many other tables, and chairs, and a very dirty floor. She could not possibly clean everything tonight. Maybe she should clean one more table, so she could sleep on it if she needed to.

So Erin cleaned another table. That was easy, too. Why not clean a third? And since she was already working, why not do all the tables? She kept going, filling her bucket again and again. After some time, every table was clean.

Then Erin looked at the chairs. "Well, I can clean those too." She wiped them one by one. When she finished, she turned to the long bar. It seemed wrong to leave it dirty when everything else was clean.

The bar was made of beautiful dark wood. As Erin wiped away the dust, the last light from outside touched its surface. The wood almost seemed to glow. The bar was long enough for many people to sit together. Erin could imagine it full of people again.

She cleaned the shelf behind the bar and the other surfaces in the room. Soon, the whole main room looked different. The clean wood reflected the light, and the empty inn felt warmer. Erin stood back and looked at what she had done. For a moment, she was proud of herself.

But there was one problem. The floor. All the dirt from the tables and chairs was now on the floor. There were wet piles of dust everywhere. Erin had no mop, and she was far too tired to start again. She pushed one dirty pile with her foot.

"Well, the tables are clean," she said. She laughed at the strange result. The tables looked good enough to eat from, but the floor was terrible. "People walk on floors," she told herself. "They don't sleep on them." That was good enough for tonight.

Erin wiped the sweat from her face and looked outside. The rain had stopped. Night had come while she was cleaning, and now the inn was almost completely dark. The black rooms beyond the main hall seemed frightening again. "Great," Erin said. "Now it's not just scary. It's really scary."

Still, the main room felt different now. Moonlight touched the clean tables and chairs. Erin had done all this work herself. In a small way, the room now felt like her own place. She wanted to believe that made it safe.

She sat down in a chair and leaned against a table. She was so tired that she could hardly move. Here she was, lost in a dangerous world and not knowing how to survive. And what had she done first? She had cleaned a room. Erin laughed softly at herself.

"At least Mom would be happy," she said. She closed her eyes. Maybe tomorrow things would be better. Maybe this was all a dream, though she did not really believe it. Her breathing grew slower as she began to fall asleep.

One last thought came into her head. She opened her mouth and whispered, "Now I'm really, really hungry."

The inn was quiet and dark. Erin's eyes were almost closed, and she was nearly asleep. Then strange words came to her in the darkness. She did not know what they meant.

In the darkness, Erin opened one eye. She looked around, then quickly sat up. "...What was that?"
